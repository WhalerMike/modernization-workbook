# Chapter 06 — DLP and Auto-Labeling

*Simulation is the pilot, and enforcement is a decision someone recorded. The check holds each policy to the stage that decision gave it.*

::: {.callout-note}
## In this chapter
The code behind the guide's [G8 DLP and endpoint DLP](../../../books/guides/information-protection-implementation.md#step-8-dlp-and-endpoint-dlp), [G9 Auto-labeling and sensitive information types](../../../books/guides/information-protection-implementation.md#step-9-auto-labeling-and-sensitive-information-types) and the Copilot DLP policy of [G11](../../../books/guides/information-protection-implementation.md#step-11-ai-data-security). You read how `Invoke-IpDlp` and `Invoke-IpAutoLabel` compare each policy's `Mode` with its planned stage, and how every DLP exception is held to the register. Lab 1 finds three faults; Lab 2 checks a test tenant mid-rollout.
:::

## Goal and audience

For the developer who owns the DLP and auto-labeling checks, and the platform owner who moves policies between stages. By the end you can say which policies enforce, which are still in simulation, which moved without approval, and which exceptions have outlived their approval.

## Start with the guide's Validate steps

### What the guide gives you

G8 lists `Get-DlpCompliancePolicy` with `Mode` and `Get-DlpComplianceRule`; the pass is that each outcome's production policy is `Enable`, retired ring policies are `Disable`, and nothing is in a test mode the rollout plan doesn't put there. G9 lists `Get-AutoSensitivityLabelPolicy`; none may say `Enable` without the policy owner's approval. [Book 20, Chapter 09](../../../books/b20-information-protection/09-auto-labeling-and-sensitive-information-types.md#how-service-side-auto-labeling-from-simulation-to-rollout) warns why: the portal can turn a policy on by itself seven days after simulation if nobody edits it.[^ip06-auto]

### What it leaves to you

- **The rollout plan as data.** Each policy and its stage, in the plan's `dlpPolicies` and `autoLabelPolicies`.
- **Exceptions with expiry.** [Book 20, Chapter 08](../../../books/b20-information-protection/08-dlp-and-endpoint-controls.md#exceptions-without-holes) puts every exception in one register with an approver and an expiry. The check reads the exceptions in the export and looks each one up.

## The code

`Test-IpStage` maps a stage to the `Mode` values Learn gives for both cmdlets:[^ip06-dlp][^ip06-auto]

| Stage | Allowed `Mode` |
|---|---|
| `simulation` | `TestWithoutNotifications` or `TestWithNotifications` |
| `notify` | `TestWithNotifications` |
| `enforce` | `Enable` |
| `retired` | `Disable` |

: Stages and modes {.striped .hover}

A policy missing from the export fails; an exported policy the plan doesn't name fails (`DLP-EXTRA`, `AUTO-EXTRA`). The message says which way it moved: `Enable` early is "enforcing without the approval the plan records"; out of `Enable` while the stage is `enforce` is "enforcement is off".

`Invoke-IpDlp` then reads every `ExceptIfRecipientDomainIs` in a rule and every `ExchangeSenderMemberOfException` on a policy, and looks each up in `templates/exceptions.csv` by kind, policy and target. No entry fails; an expired entry fails; `EXC-EXPIRED` lists every register entry past expiry, whatever it covers. `Invoke-IpAutoLabel` also checks that each custom type the plan names is in the catalog.

Tag `ch06` covers the three faults and an exception with no register entry.

::: {.callout-warning}
## Illustrative code
Lab 1 reads fictional fixtures. The check reads modes and exceptions; it doesn't measure whether a policy works. Book 20's synthetic tests (Chapter 07) and the DLP alert dispositions do that. DLP simulation mode's dashboard isn't listed for GCC; the stage `simulation` relies only on the `Mode` value.
:::

## Verified facts

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| DLP `Mode` | `Enable`, `Disable`, `TestWithNotifications`, `TestWithoutNotifications` | [^ip06-dlp] |
| Auto-labeling `Mode` | The same four (and `PendingDeletion`); the portal option to turn a policy on after seven days | [^ip06-auto] |
| DLP and auto-labeling in GCC | DLP for files, email, Teams and endpoints, and service-side auto-labeling, Available; simulation mode not listed | [^ip06-gcc] |
| Exceptions | Rule-level `ExceptIfRecipientDomainIs`; policy-level `ExchangeSenderMemberOfException` | [^ip06-rule] |

## Federal requirements and evidence

| Requirement it supports | KSI | What the ISSO keeps |
|---|---|---|
| AC-4, MP-7, SI-4, IR-4 (DLP); AC-16, CM-12 (auto-labeling) | KSI-SVC-SIN, KSI-SVC-RUD, KSI-MLA-RVL; KSI-SVC-ACM | `results.csv`, `exceptions.csv`, the exception register |

The checks support these controls; the agency's assessor decides whether a control is satisfied.

## Dangers

| Danger | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| An auto-labeling policy turns itself on | Leave the portal option unselected; `AUTO` rows and drift catch it |
| An exception outlives its agreement | `EXC` rows by expiry; `EXC-EXPIRED` for the whole register |
| A Copilot policy rolled back for a test and left there | It's a planned stage like any other; drift raises it to its own signal |

: Dangers in the DLP and auto-labeling checks {.striped .hover}
