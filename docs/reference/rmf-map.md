# RMF Map {.unnumbered}

An ISSO, a control assessor or an authorizing official reads the workbook with one question: where is the Risk Management Framework, and what does each step leave behind? The books answer it, but chapter by chapter. This page puts the answer in one place. For each of the seven steps of NIST SP 800-37 Rev. 2 it lists the tasks, the outputs NIST names, where the workbook treats each one, the evidence the program and its kits produce, and where the workbook stops.[^sp80037]

The [Federal Framework Crosswalk's](federal-framework-crosswalk.md) RMF row is derived from this map's marks; where they differ, this map wins. The [M-19-17 and FICAM Crosswalk](m-19-17-ficam-crosswalk.md) does the same job for identity.

::: {.callout-important}
## FISMA isn't a level

FISMA is a statute. It requires each agency to run an agency-wide information security program, with testing "no less than annually," and gives the agency head, the CIO and the senior agency information security officer their duties.[^fisma] It defines no impact levels. "FISMA Moderate" is shorthand for three separate facts: the system's FIPS 199 categorization rates confidentiality, integrity and availability;[^fips199] FIPS 200's high-water mark makes the system moderate when at least one is moderate and none is high;[^fips200] and the SP 800-53B moderate baseline was then selected and tailored. A FedRAMP certification class is a fourth fact, about a provider's service, not the agency's system. [Book 14, Chapter 01](../books/b14-compliance/01-proof-not-a-promise.md#what-moderate-means) traces all of them.
:::

## How to read this map

- **Task names** are NIST's, from SP 800-37 Rev. 2, Chapter 3 and Appendix E, read November 2, 2026; chapter links and anchors re-checked November 6, 2026.[^sp80037] The organization-level Prepare tasks are P-1 to P-7 and the system-level ones P-8 to P-18; the other steps run C-1 to C-3, S-1 to S-6, I-1 and I-2, A-1 to A-6, R-1 to R-5 and M-1 to M-7.
- **Coverage** is judged from what the chapters actually say:
  - **Covered**: the chapter tells the reader what the task requires, who does it, and what evidence shows it done.
  - **Partial**: the requirement is explained, but the workbook stops short of the artifact, or treats only part of the task.
  - **Partial by design**: the workbook gives the method, a neutral fill-in template and a Copilot prompt, and stops there on purpose, because the content or the decision is the agency's. The template is the most a public workbook can supply, so this mark doesn't move to Covered.
  - **Not covered**: nothing in the books does the task.
- **Evidence** names the artifact an assessor would read. Where a kit in the repository's [`examples`](https://github.com/WhalerMike/modernization-workbook/tree/main/examples) folder produces it, the kit is named. Every kit is illustrative: tested against fixtures, not proven against a production tenant.
- **Design guidance** is marked as such. The cadences, dispositions and freshness limits in Book 14 are the program's design, not a federal requirement; the books say so where they set them.

## Step 2 — Categorize

The workbook assumes a moderate system and says why, and [Book 14, Appendix F](../books/b14-compliance/appendix-f-categorization-walkthrough.md) then does the process once: one invented system, walked from its SP 800-60 information types to a FIPS 199 high-water mark and an approval record, with a fill-in template and a Copilot prompt. It's an illustration. The example's adjustments are invented, two of its type mappings are judgments the appendix flags for confirmation, and a real categorization stays the agency's, so all three tasks stop at Partial by design.

**OSCAL: two models of four.** The drift pipeline's `oscal_export.py` turns an evidence package into OSCAL 1.2.3 Assessment Results and a POA&M, both validated against NIST's published schemas, and [lab step 10](../books/b19-developer-workbook/09-drift-pipelines-and-evidence.md#federal-requirements-and-evidence) runs it. It's tested against fixtures only. No assessor's tooling has accepted its output, its check-to-control map is a draft to review, and it reads dispositions from an acceptance register without deciding any. No kit writes an OSCAL system security plan or assessment plan, so the `import-ssp` and `import-ap` links in the output point at files the agency supplies. Book 14, Chapter 11 describes all four models; producing those two is still the agency's work, or its governance tool's.

## Not covered, in one list

No task is marked Not covered. What the workbook still lacks falls into three groups.

**Missing artifacts the workbook could add:**

- **OSCAL system security plan and assessment plan generation** (S-4, A-2, M-4). The drift pipeline writes Assessment Results and a POA&M; nothing writes the other two, and the plans aren't regenerated.
- **A single tailored baseline** with every organization-defined parameter value (S-2).
- **A CSF Organizational Profile** (P-4, a task NIST makes optional). The books map the CSF Functions, not a Profile.
- **Internal reporting of the authorization decision** (R-5) beyond the FedRAMP notice.

**Stops at a template by design.** The workbook gives the method and a Copilot prompt; the content is the agency's:

- The risk management strategy and risk tolerance (P-2), impact-level prioritization, which NIST makes optional (P-6), and the system-level risk assessment (P-14): [Book 14, Appendix G](../books/b14-compliance/appendix-g-prepare-step-templates.md).
- The information types, system description, categorization and approval of a real system (P-12, C-1, C-2, C-3): [Book 14, Appendix F](../books/b14-compliance/appendix-f-categorization-walkthrough.md).
- The assessment plan and the assessment report (A-2, A-4): [Book 14, Appendix C](../books/b14-compliance/appendix-c-security-assessment-plan-template.md) and [Appendix D](../books/b14-compliance/appendix-d-security-assessment-report-template.md).

**Partial for other reasons:**

- **Agency-level records** the program's artifacts only feed: the organization-wide risk assessment (P-3), the mission analysis (P-8), the full stakeholder list (P-9), the life cycle of each information type (P-13), requirements allocation beyond provider, agency or shared (P-17), and system registration (P-18).
- **Decisions with no template:** plan approval (S-6) and the risk determination (R-2).
- **The independent assessment** (A-3). Kit runs are self-assessment evidence the assessor weighs.
