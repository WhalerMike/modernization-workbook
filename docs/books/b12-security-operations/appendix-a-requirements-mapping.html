Appendix A — Requirements Mapping: Controls, Mandates and Books

A compliance-enabling map, not a compliance claim: the NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 controls security operations asserts (AU, CA-7, CM-6/7/8, RA-5, SI-2/3/4, IR-4/5, SC-7), with their NIST and FedRAMP baselines, and the mandates behind them (OMB M-26-14, BOD 26-04 and the KEV catalog, BOD 23-01, BOD 25-01, TIC 3.0, CISA incident notification, FedRAMP’s 2026 rules, NIST’s RMF and continuous monitoring guidance), each mapped to the Book 12 chapter that carries it and to the other workbook books; then the requirements that are cited but don’t say what is claimed, and the sources.

A control says what an assessor will look for, a mandate says when, and neither says who; a map is useful only if it keeps all three apart.

NoteIn this appendix

The NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 controls that security operations asserts, with their titles, what each asks, their baselines in NIST SP 800-53B and in FedRAMP’s 2026 Rev5 classes, the Book 12 chapter that carries each and the other workbook books that close part of it. Then the federal mandates: OMB M-26-14, BOD 26-04 and the KEV catalog, BOD 23-01, BOD 25-01, TIC 3.0, CISA’s incident notification guidelines, FedRAMP’s Consolidated Rules for 2026 and NIST’s RMF and continuous monitoring guidance. Then the requirements that are often cited for this book’s subject but don’t say what is claimed, and the source list.

ImportantCompliance-enabling, not a compliance claim

This appendix helps an agency reuse the workbook’s design and evidence in its own Agency Logging Plan, continuous monitoring strategy and system security plans. It doesn’t claim that following the workbook satisfies any requirement or control. Control selection, implementation statements and assessment belong to the agency and its assessors, and the authorizing official alone accepts residual risk. Requirement wording is summarized; the cited documents govern. Every source in this appendix was checked on October 21, 2026.

How to read the tables

  • Baselines gives two values. The first is the NIST SP 800-53B baseline that includes the control (L, M, H, or none), checked against NIST’s OSCAL baseline profiles.1 The second is the FedRAMP 2026 Rev5 classes whose baselines include it (B, C, D, or none), from FedRAMP’s control reference; Book 10 records Microsoft 365 GCC’s certification as Class C, Moderate.2 The agency’s own systems use the baseline its categorization selects.
  • Carried in names the Book 12 chapters that design or evidence the control.
  • Other books names the workbook books that build part of it.
  • Control identifiers and titles are from SP 800-53 Rev. 5, release 5.2.0, checked against the OSCAL catalog and the CSRC publication page.3

Requirements that are cited but don’t say what is claimed

The source volumes behind this book were drafted during a year of rescissions and new directives. These are the claims most worth checking before they reach a plan.

Claimed requirement What the record shows
BOD 22-01 requires agencies to remediate KEV entries by their catalog due dates BOD 22-01 was revoked by BOD 26-04 on June 10, 2026, along with BOD 19-02. The KEV catalog continues under BOD 26-04, whose timelines depend on exposure, KEV status, automatability and technical impact.28
A 14-day remediation tier for KEV vulnerabilities on internet-facing systems is “at least as tight as” the federal deadline Under BOD 26-04, a KEV-listed vulnerability on a publicly exposed asset has 3 days in three of four cases and 14 days only when exploitation is neither automatable nor gives total control.29 A 14-day tier for all of them is looser than the directive.
M-26-14 sets 12 months of active storage plus 18 months of cold storage, in EL0–EL3 tiers Those were M-21-31’s figures and tiers. M-26-14 sets 6 months searchable and 1 year retrievable, and a five-level maturity model.30
AU-12(1), AU-6(4) and SI-4(16) close at the FedRAMP Moderate gate In NIST SP 800-53B, AU-12(1) is High only and AU-6(4) and SI-4(16) are in no baseline. In FedRAMP’s 2026 Rev5 reference, AU-12(1) and AU-6(4) are Class D only; SI-4(16) is Classes C and D.3132
FedRAMP 20x KSI automation queries an agency’s Sentinel tables and fails a workspace with no enabled rules FedRAMP’s indicators state outcomes, such as a SIEM “used and persistently reviewed,” and bind cloud service providers seeking certification. They specify no query and no agency pipeline.33
The SOC is an RMF role with its own risk authority SP 800-37 defines no SOC role; only the authorizing official accepts risk. M-26-14 names the top-level SOC as the place logs must be available, not as a decision maker.3435
A data lake or archive tier satisfies the 6-month “searchable” requirement for detection sources M-26-14 defines searchable as usable for detections without preparation. Microsoft says logs ingested only into the Sentinel data lake tier don’t generate alerts.3637 The program keeps detection sources in the analytics tier (Chapter 12).
