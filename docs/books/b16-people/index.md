Every book before this one changes what people do. The help desk verifies before it resets. Owners approve access instead of inheriting it. Stewards keep the record, ISSOs assemble evidence, engineers build to a baseline, and users sign in with a PIV card, a passkey or Windows Hello instead of a password and a push prompt. Tools enforce the new model, but people run it, and a program that trains last pays twice: once for the training and once for the rework, the tickets and the exceptions that untrained staff create. Book 16 treats training as part of the program, not an afterthought. It sets out what federal rules actually require: FISMA's duty to have trained staff, OPM's 5 CFR 930.301 and the training evaluation rules in Part 410, the NICE Framework's work roles and federal cybersecurity workforce coding. It then lays out two tracks, compliance and implementation, mapped module by module to the books that teach them, and assessments that test whether people can do the work, not just whether they finished a course. Vendor training and safe labs come next, along with end-user training timed to each migration wave and the people who own readiness. Readiness is measured and evidenced at each of the program's gates.
