## The brief

<!-- form-start -->
**The point:** an orphaned account isn't an administrator's mistake. It's the guaranteed output of systems that share no event channel with HR, so sweeps remove a batch and the accounts come back. Fix the wiring, not the symptom.

**One leaver, day by day**

| Day | What happens |
|:---|:-------------------------|
| −14 | HR records the separation. No other system is listening. |
| 0 | The separation takes effect. The account, application logins and badge are still live. |
| +3 | A ticket disables the directory account. Nine application accounts stay enabled. |
| +45 | A review flags two of them. Nobody owns the other seven. |
| +180 | A penetration test signs in with one. The gap is now an incident. |

**Why it reaches you**

- NIST SP 800-53 expects account management to align with personnel termination and transfer (AC-2 item l), and PS-5 covers transfers.
- Access reviews stay as the check (AC-2 item j), but they sample state. They can't observe a transition when it happens.

**What changes**

- HR-driven identity gives those systems the event channel they're missing.
- When automation turns on, it turns on leaver first, because revocation is where the risk and the evidence value are.
- The separation-to-block and separation-to-revocation intervals are measured from day one. That's your before and after.

**What we ask of you**

- Set the control requirements: MFA strength, the Conditional Access baseline and logging.
- Say which assessment findings are priority one for Gate G0.
- Support the four-week pre-flight, and agree the leaver measure.

**Our number**

Days for a leaver to lose access, from the HR record to the last account disabled: \[days\], from \[count\] separations
