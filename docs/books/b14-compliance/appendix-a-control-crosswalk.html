Appendix A — Control Crosswalk

The NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 controls the Federal Modernization Program addresses, family by family: each control’s identifier and title, its NIST SP 800-53B baselines and its FedRAMP 2026 class membership as separate facts, the workbook book and chapter that carries it, and whether the program’s design closes it, partly closes it or leaves it open.

A crosswalk row says where a control is built and whether its proof exists, not that an assessor has accepted either.

NoteIn this appendix

The reverse index for Chapter 03: for every NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 control the program addresses, its title, the NIST SP 800-53B baselines that select it, the FedRAMP 2026 classes whose Rev5 baselines include it, the workbook chapter that builds or operates it, and its status in the program’s design. Then what the crosswalk shows when the columns are read together, where it corrects its source, and the sources it was checked against.

ImportantA coverage index, not a compliance claim

A row here points to the chapter that treats a control as its subject. It doesn’t claim the control is satisfied, inherited or shared on any system; that depends on the system’s boundary and on the assessor’s determination, and the authorizing official alone accepts residual risk. The status column is the program’s design, not an assessment result. Control identifiers, titles, baselines and class memberships were checked on October 21, 2026.

How to read the crosswalk

  • Control and title. Identifiers and titles are from SP 800-53 Rev. 5, release 5.2.0, which NIST issued on August 27, 2025.1 Every identifier and title was checked against the release 5.2.0 export of NIST’s Cybersecurity and Privacy Reference Tool on CSRC and against NIST’s OSCAL catalog for the same release.2 Enhancements carry the enhancement’s own title; the parent control is the row above or the base identifier.
  • NIST SP 800-53B. The NIST baselines that select the control: L, M and H for the Low, Moderate and High security baselines, and “Privacy” for the privacy baseline, which applies to systems whatever their impact level.3 Release 5.2.0 of SP 800-53B made no changes to the baselines.4 Membership was read from NIST’s OSCAL baseline profiles and cross-checked against the CSRC export; the two agree for every row.56
  • FedRAMP 2026 class. The FedRAMP Rev5 certification classes whose baselines include the control (B, C or D), read from the “FedRAMP Rev5 Baselines” line on each control in FedRAMP’s Full Rev5 Control Reference.7 FedRAMP’s February 2026 notice set Class B to include the former Li-SaaS and Low baselines, Class C the Moderate baseline and Class D the High baseline.8 “None” means the reference shows no class for that control. Keep this column apart from the NIST one: they often disagree, and the difference matters (What the crosswalk shows).
  • Workbook chapter. The chapter in Books 00 to 13 where the program builds, configures or operates the control, written as book and chapter (B12 Ch07 is Book 12, Chapter 07). A dash means no book from 00 to 13 carries it; the family’s Book 14 chapter, named above each table, describes what the agency has to establish.
  • Status (design). The program’s own design position, in three values:
    • Closed: a workbook chapter builds the mechanism, and the program’s design records a machine-checked proof for it in the authoritative record. A control closes only when the mechanism exists and its proof is recorded (Chapter 04).
    • Partly closed: one half is in place. Usually a workbook chapter builds the mechanism, but the program doesn’t yet record a proof for the control; the claim rests on the chapter’s prose and the evidence it describes.
    • Open: the program describes the control, in Book 14 or in the Federal Compliance volumes, but no workbook book from 00 to 13 builds its mechanism and no proof is recorded. Most open rows are policies, plans and program-level functions the agency has to establish itself.

The program’s design moves a row from partly closed to closed by recording its proof, not by writing more about it. Treat every status as dated: a closed row reopens when its evidence stops arriving (Book 13, Chapter 07).

What the crosswalk shows

Read the columns together and four patterns stand out.

NIST baseline and FedRAMP class are different facts. FedRAMP’s class baselines add to NIST’s, so a control can be in a FedRAMP class without being in the matching NIST baseline:910

Control NIST SP 800-53B FedRAMP 2026 class What it means
SC-8, SC-8(1), SC-28 M H B C D FedRAMP asks a Class B service for transmission and at-rest protection that NIST’s Low baseline doesn’t select
SI-2(3), SI-4(16) none C D Remediation timeframes and correlated monitoring are FedRAMP additions at Class C; an agency system categorized Moderate selects neither unless the agency tailors them in
AU-6(4) none D Central review and analysis is a FedRAMP High addition
AU-12(1), SC-12(1) H D Both are High-only in both schemes; neither is a Moderate requirement
AU-10 H D Non-repudiation is High-only in both; the program builds it anyway, as provenance for the record (Book 13, Chapter 04)
CA-5 L M H none NIST selects the plan of action and milestones at every level; FedRAMP’s reference lists no class for it

The last row reports what the reference shows; it isn’t a reason to drop a POA&M. For an agency system, the NIST baseline its categorization selects is the starting point, and FedRAMP’s class tells the agency what was assessed in a provider’s service.11

Some controls the program addresses are in no baseline. AT-2(1), AT-3(3), CA-7(3), IA-3(1), IA-9 and SI-4(7) are in neither NIST’s nor FedRAMP’s baselines.1213 The program addresses them because they name exactly what it builds. An agency that wants them in its plan has to tailor them in, and shouldn’t cite them as baseline requirements.

Program management and privacy controls sit outside the class baselines. None of the PM or PT controls here carries a FedRAMP class, and the PM and PT family pages in FedRAMP’s reference show no class line at all.14 On the NIST side, the PT controls and PM-7, PM-9, PM-11 and PM-14 are in the privacy baseline only, and the other PM controls are in no baseline.15 These are the agency’s own obligations: no cloud provider’s certification can supply them.

The open rows cluster in the governance families. Contingency planning, program management, PII processing and transparency, supply chain risk management and awareness and training are almost entirely open, because no infrastructure book builds a contingency plan, a supplier agreement or a training record. That’s why this book carries them: Chapter 05 to Chapter 09 describe what the agency has to establish and what evidence each control should leave. The technical families are mostly closed or partly closed, and the partly closed rows are the program’s own queue: each needs its proof recorded, not more design.

Where this appendix corrects its source

The control-by-control mapping behind this appendix was drafted in July 2026, against FedRAMP’s 20x pilot vocabulary and a book-and-volume layout that the workbook has since replaced.

Source said What the record shows
Each control maps to a “FedRAMP 20x KSI” such as KSI-IAM, KSI-MLA or KSI-022 FedRAMP’s 2026 Key Security Indicators are 46 outcome statements in ten themes, each with its own identifier (for example KSI-CED-RAT for reviewing training) and its own list of related controls; no indicator is numbered KSI-022, and a theme prefix such as KSI-IAM names a group, not an indicator.16 The indicators bind cloud service providers seeking certification, not agencies. This appendix drops the column; Book 13, Appendix A maps the governance controls to the current indicators.
A control is “addressed” when a volume or book of the compliance series treats it The index now names the workbook chapter that builds the control, and says separately whether the program’s design records its proof. Many rows the source listed as addressed have no build chapter; they are open here.
Control titles as written in each volume Every title was rechecked against release 5.2.0. The source’s titles were right in substance; capitalization now follows NIST’s, for example “Multi-factor,” “Re-authentication” and “Denial-of-service Protection.”17
Baseline position not stated Both NIST baselines and FedRAMP classes are given for every row, as separate columns.
