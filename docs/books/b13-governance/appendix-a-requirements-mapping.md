# Appendix A — Requirements Mapping: Controls, Mandates and Books

*Every control in this appendix assumes a record that stays true between assessments; none of them supplies one.*

::: {.callout-note}
## In this appendix
The NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 controls that Book 13's governance model relies on, with their titles, what each asks, their baselines in NIST SP 800-53B and in FedRAMP's 2026 Rev5 classes, the FedRAMP 2026 Key Security Indicators that cite them, the Book 13 chapter that carries each and the other workbook books that close part of it. Then the risk management tasks and federal mandates behind the book's argument: NIST SP 800-37 Rev. 2, SP 800-137, OMB Circular A-130, OMB M-24-15, the FedRAMP Consolidated Rules for 2026, and CISA BOD 25-01 and BOD 26-04. Then the requirements that the source material cites but that don't say what is claimed, and the source list.
:::

::: {.callout-important}
## Compliance-enabling, not a compliance claim
This appendix helps an agency reuse the workbook's governance design and evidence in its own continuous monitoring strategy, system security plans and authorization packages. It doesn't claim that following the workbook satisfies any requirement or control. Control selection, implementation statements and assessment belong to the agency and its assessors, and the authorizing official alone accepts residual risk. Requirement wording is summarized; the cited documents govern. Every source in this appendix was checked on October 21, 2026.
:::

## How to read the tables

- **NIST SP 800-53B** gives the NIST baselines that include the control (L, M, H, or none), read from NIST's OSCAL Low, Moderate and High baseline profiles.[^aA-53b] **FedRAMP 2026** gives the FedRAMP Rev5 classes whose baselines include it (B, C, D, or none), read from the "FedRAMP Rev5 Baselines" line on each control in FedRAMP's 2026 control reference.[^aA-fedramp-controls] The two columns are verified separately and don't always agree. FedRAMP adds controls to NIST's baselines, and the difference matters for this book: CM-3(2) and SI-2(2) are in NIST's Moderate baseline, but CM-5(1) and CM-6(1) are NIST High only and CM-3(1) is NIST High only, while FedRAMP puts CM-5(1) and CM-6(1) in Classes C and D and CM-3(1) in Class D only. "Required at Moderate" therefore means nothing until it says which: an agency system categorized Moderate uses the NIST baseline its categorization selects, and a FedRAMP Class C service uses FedRAMP's.
- **Indicators** lists the FedRAMP 2026 Key Security Indicators whose "Related SP 800-53 Controls" include the control, as published on October 21, 2026. FedRAMP revised many of these mappings on October 13, 2026, so check the page before relying on a row. The indicators bind cloud service providers seeking certification, not agencies.[^aA-fedramp-ksi]
- **Carried in** names the Book 13 chapters that design or evidence the control. Chapters are named by their file names in the book's plan.
- **Other books** names the workbook books that build part of it. Planned books without pages yet are named and linked to the [workbook index](../index.md).
- Control identifiers and titles are from SP 800-53 Rev. 5, release 5.2.0, checked against NIST's control catalog export on CSRC and the OSCAL catalog.[^aA-53]

Directives bind agencies through the Federal Information Security Modernization Act: a binding operational directive is a compulsory direction, and agencies are required to comply with it.[^aA-bod2501] OMB's FedRAMP memorandum works differently. It sets a presumption agencies must apply, and it leaves the authorization decision with the agency: FedRAMP's own rules require agencies to complete the ATO process for systems that use certified services.[^aA-m2415][^aA-fedramp-agu]

## What non-compliance exposes

None of the documents cited in this appendix sets a monetary penalty for an agency. The consequences they name are of three kinds, and all three are written into the record the book's governance model produces.

| Authority | What makes it binding | What a failure produces |
|---|---|---|
| CISA BOD 25-01 and BOD 26-04 | Compulsory direction under 44 U.S.C. § 3554(a)(1)(B)(ii)[^aA-bod2501] | Reported deviations and missed timelines, visible to CISA through the reporting each directive requires |
| The authorization | A risk acceptance with terms, conditions and a termination date the authorizing official can change at any time; a denial is also a decision[^aA-sp80037] | A shortened, conditioned or denied authorization |
| OMB Circular A-130 | Initial authorization before operation, reauthorization on a time- or event-driven basis, a continuous monitoring strategy[^aA-a130] | Reauthorization triggered by events, not just by the calendar |
| FedRAMP 2026 Agency Use rules | Agencies must notify FedRAMP when certification data raises concerns likely to result in rescinding their ATO[^aA-fedramp-agu] | A notification to FedRAMP, and the ATO decision that follows it |

The record of non-closure is the exposure that matters most in practice. An assessment tool that runs faithfully against an estate nobody remediates produces an accurate, lengthening list of open findings, and every auditor who asks can read it.

## Requirements that are cited but don't say what is claimed

The source material behind this book was drafted while FedRAMP's rules, OMB's FedRAMP policy and CISA's vulnerability directive all changed. These claims are the ones most worth checking before they reach a plan.

| Claimed requirement | What the record shows |
|---|---|
| CM-3(2), CM-5(1), CM-6(1) and SI-2(2) are the Rev. 5 Moderate baseline enhancements that mandate automation | In NIST SP 800-53B, CM-3(2) and SI-2(2) are Moderate and High, but CM-5(1) and CM-6(1) are High only, as is CM-3(1). In FedRAMP's 2026 Rev5 reference all four are in Classes C and D.[^aA-53b][^aA-fedramp-controls] The claim holds for a FedRAMP Class C service, not for an agency system categorized Moderate. |
| FedRAMP's vulnerability rules set remediation timeframes of 4 to 192 days | The timeframes depend on certification class. At Class D they run from 12 hours to 192 days, at Class C from 2 days to 192 days, and at Classes A and B from 4 days to 192 days. They are "SHOULD" statements for providers, not agency obligations.[^aA-fedramp-vdr] |
| SI-2 feeds KSI-SVC and KSI-MLA; RA-5, CA-2 and CA-5 feed KSI-MLA; IA-5(2) and SC-17 feed KSI-IAM | On FedRAMP's page, SI-2 is cited by KSI-CMT-VTD and SI-2(2) by KSI-SVC-EIS. RA-5 is cited by KSI-SCR-MON, CA-2 by KSI-PIY-RIS, and CA-5 by no indicator. SC-17 is cited by KSI-SVC-ASM, and IA-5(2) by KSI-SVC-ASM and three IAM indicators.[^aA-fedramp-ksi] |
| Eight reciprocity indicators, KSI-RECIP-001 to 008, are FedRAMP 20x Key Security Indicators | FedRAMP publishes 46 indicators in ten themes and none is a reciprocity indicator.[^aA-fedramp-ksi] The reciprocity checks are the program's own health checks and should be labeled that way ([Chapter 11](11-reciprocity-and-one-authorization.md)). |
| One controlling ATO covers every consuming agency, and each agency's reciprocity record is not a new authorization | Under SP 800-37, a consuming organization's authorizing official issues an authorization to use, carrying the same responsibility as an authorization to operate.[^aA-sp80037] M-24-15 has agencies presume the FedRAMP package adequate "for their use in issuing an authorization to operate," and FedRAMP's rules require agencies to complete the ATO process.[^aA-m2415][^aA-fedramp-agu] A signed reciprocity record is good evidence for that decision. It doesn't replace it. |
| BOD 25-01 has five obligations, and the fifth, "remediate deviations," is the one the directive is for | The directive's required actions are tenant inventory, tool deployment with continuous or quarterly reporting, implementing the mandatory policies and their future updates, and meeting the baselines for new tenants before an ATO. Remediating deviations is the purpose the directive states in its background, achieved through the mandatory policies, and authorizing officials may accept risk for deviations.[^aA-bod2501] The source's practical point stands: four of those actions can be met without closing a single finding. |
| Under BOD 26-04, agency policy updates were due August 7, 2026 and full compliance December 7, 2026 | The policy review is Phase I and took effect on issue, June 10, 2026. Phase II falls 60 days after issue and Phase III, the remediation timelines and asset tagging, 180 days after issue, which is December 7, 2026.[^aA-bod2604] |
| Agencies "must" review each provider's Ongoing Certification Report | The Collaborative Continuous Monitoring rule CCM-AGM-ROR says MUST; the Agency Use summary rule AGU-USE-ROR says SHOULD for the same review.[^aA-fedramp-ccm][^aA-fedramp-agu] Treat the review as required and plan for it; the workbook follows the stricter rule. |
| KSI-CMT-RMV forbids modifying a resource in place | The indicator says changes are executed through redeployment of version-controlled resources "wherever reasonable."[^aA-fedramp-ksi] It is a strong preference with room for a documented exception, which is how [Appendix B](appendix-b-ticket-to-machine-reference.md) treats it. |
